This bride wore five custom looks for her weeklong Vietnamese wedding—then raved barefoot under the stars

Across Da Nang, Hoi An, Ho Chi Minh City and the Amanoi, these newlyweds gathered friends and family from around the globe for a celebration of heritage, family traditions, and a very late night on the beach. Wedding photographer: Pablo Laguía

Rahemma Azwar
September 15, 2026 - 3:41PM

For Tiffany Le and Alexander Fleming, their destination wedding was never planned to be contained to just a single day of celebrations. When 130 guests travelled from across the world to the Amanoi on Vietnam’s rugged southeastern coast on a late spring day, they weren’t simply gathering for a ceremony of holy matrimony. Unbeknownst to them, friends and family were embarking on a week-long celebration of culture, food, music, and inevitably, a little chaos.

“It truly felt like a huge friends and family vacation as opposed to a wedding,” says Tiffany. Alexander shared the same appetite for adventure. Their wedding was in many ways a reflection of the life they built together: sophisticated but irreverent, and always ready for the next party.

The pair first met at Harvard Business School in 2017, where they were assigned in the same section. Their first impressions, however, were hardly romantic. Tiffany, who grew up in California after being born in Vietnam, saw herself as a “west coast” girl; Alex had attended Brown University and Cambridge and rowed crew at both schools. “To be honest, I did not like Alex at first,” Tiffany admits. Alex, meanwhile, says he was trying to impress her with a “too cool for school” attitude—an approach that did not exactly work.

But repeated encounters eventually brought them closer, and as many love stories before them go, friendship came first. “We ended up becoming best friends over time once we realised how much we had in common,” Tiffany says. Hiking, fishing, live music, food, and nights out all became shared passions and rituals for the pair. “‘Work hard, play hard’ is both of our life mottos.”

Alex’s proposal was exceptionally sui generis. In December 2021, he took Tiffany for an ordinary-seeming date night of omakase, followed by cocktails at their favourite New York bar, Death & Co. In a secluded booth, he gave her a leather-bound folio embossed with her initials. Inside were six original paintings he had secretly created, each depicting a significant memory from their relationship.

Next came a checklist—a nod to their shared obsession with organisation—with one final achievement left unchecked: “Marry the love of my life.” The final page was a letter Alex had written in Vietnamese to Tiffany’s parents asking for their permission to marry her. “My parents and my Vietnamese culture are everything to me, so this gesture was incredibly meaningful” Tiffany says.

When it came time to choose a wedding venue, the couple searched for more than a year. Then Tiffany had an idea—why not return to Amanoi, where they had stayed on a trip two years earlier and immediately fallen in love? Hidden in the mountains overlooking the sea, the resort offered the dramatic natural surroundings yet understated architecture that they were looking for. It also carried special significance for Tiffany. “It really made me feel so rooted to my culture,” she says.

The wedding week, planned and led by The Wedding Bliss, began with 80 guests travelling through Da Nang, Hoi An, and Ho Chi Minh City. There were lantern-lit riverside gatherings, beach parties, cultural experiences, coffee tastings, dinners, and nights spent dancing. On Friday, guests gathered for a traditional Vietnamese wedding ceremony before heading to a night market welcome dinner, where everyone was dressed in red.

The setting was transformed with hanging lanterns, bamboo food carts, custom Vietnamese-patterned tablecloths, and a dramatic floral-printed curtain. Each guest received a welcome card featuring a custom illustration of themselves wearing traditional Vietnamese áo dài. Saturday brought a Western ceremony on a secluded coral beach, where a natural rock formation became the altar. The couple deliberately kept the styling simple. “We tried to keep decor pared down to feel as if it all came from the earth and surroundings,” Tiffany says.

The bride’s ceremony dress, created over eight months by Australian designer About Phaedra, was made from hand-shirred silk, silk organza, satin, gimp, and fragments of silk tulle. Hand-sewn embroidery and cascading pleats gave the dress an almost elemental quality, inspired by the rippling movements of water. The bride’s other looks celebrated both sides of the couple’s heritage. Friday’s ceremony featured a custom Vietnamese áo dài by Xeo Xo, while her Friday-night Zimmermann red silk-tulle mini dress complemented the night market theme. For Saturday’s reception, she changed into a custom feathered gown by Vietnamese designer Thai Huy, before finishing the night in a sculptural Rachel Gilbert mini dress for the tropical trance beach rave.

Even cocktail hour had a personal flair. Tiffany, Alex, and Alex’s brother David spent weeks developing 18 custom cocktails, testing recipes in their home kitchens. The reception drinks were presented in leather-bound booklets stamped with an old fashioned glass, a flamingo, and a monkey—a playful emblem of the couple. 

Then came the rain. An hour before the ceremony, a storm threatened to derail everything. Instead, it became one of the couple’s defining memories of their nuptials. Tiffany, who had never planned to say vows publicly, found herself overwhelmed by the moment. “At the altar, looking at him and then looking out at the sea of family and friends as a light rain was falling, I was suddenly filled with so much love and gratitude that I grabbed the mic and said impromptu vows on the spot,” she recalls. “The joy and love was bursting out of me for Alex, for my family, for my friends, for my life.”

Later, the newlyweds ran into the ocean, clothes and all, while their guests embraced the downpour. By nightfall, the celebration had shifted once more. The reception dance floor became “Club Le Tiff,” while the beach afterparty was christened “Flemingo Lounge.” Alex wore a custom embroidered shirt reading “Let’s Fleming Le,” and barefoot guests danced beneath the moonlight and stars and eventually once more, the rain.
